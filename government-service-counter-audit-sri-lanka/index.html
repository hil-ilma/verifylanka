Government and Public Service Counter Audits in Sri Lanka

Citizens use public counters because they have to, not because they choose to. We check how long they wait, whether they get clear answers, and whether service is offered in the language they need.

What we check

What our auditors look at

01

Queue and waiting time

Time from arrival to being served and how the queue is managed.

02

Clarity of forms and information

Whether forms, notices and instructions are easy to understand.

03

Courtesy and helpfulness

Greeting, patience and willingness to explain.

04

Language service

Whether service is available in Sinhala, Tamil and English as promised.

05

Accessibility

Ramps, seating, signs and help for elderly and disabled visitors.

06

Service charter adherence

Whether promised processing times and standards are met.

Why it matters

What you cannot see from head office

Public offices serve everyone, so small problems affect many people. A clear picture of the citizen experience is the first step to improving it, and it supports service charters and reform programmes.

How it works

From brief to report

Auditors act as ordinary citizens with a simple request, such as collecting a certificate or paying a bill. We score each office and report patterns across districts.

We cover every district of Sri Lanka, including smaller towns and remote areas, and we can work in Sinhala, Tamil and English.
